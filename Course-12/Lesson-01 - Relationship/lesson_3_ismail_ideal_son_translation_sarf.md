# 2. التَّرْجَمَةُ الإِنْجِلِيزِيَّةُ الكَامِلَةُ
## Complete Natural English Translation

Just as the father, Luqman the Wise, presented the highest example of fatherhood, Ishmael—peace be upon him—presented the highest example of being a dutiful son. A wonderful account in the Noble Qur’an confirms this.

Ishmael was a righteous son who was exceptionally dutiful to his father, Abraham—peace be upon him. His devotion to his father reached such a degree that he agreed to allow his father to sacrifice him in fulfillment of a dream that Abraham had seen while asleep.

Allah blessed this mutual obedience and understanding between the son and his father. He rewarded them with a great ram to be sacrificed instead of the son and relieved the father’s heart by saving his son after that severe test. In this way, Allah rewards parents and children who excel in goodness, such as Abraham and Ishmael—peace be upon them both.

Allah Most High says:

> When the boy reached the age at which he could work alongside his father, Abraham said, “My dear son, I see in a dream that I am sacrificing you, so consider what you think.” He replied, “My dear father, do what you are commanded. You will find me, if Allah wills, among those who are patient.” When they had both submitted and Abraham laid him down upon his forehead, We called to him, “O Abraham, you have fulfilled the vision.” In this way We reward those who do good. This was certainly the clear test, and We ransomed him with a great sacrifice.

The Noble Qur’an also records that Ishmael participated with his father in acts of remembrance and in building the Sacred House, which Allah made a place to which people repeatedly return and a place of safety. During the construction, both father and son prayed to their Lord.

Allah Most High says:

> When Abraham and Ishmael were raising the foundations of the House, they prayed, “Our Lord, accept this from us. You alone are the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing. Our Lord, make both of us submissive to You, and make from our descendants a community submissive to You. Show us our rites of worship and accept our repentance. You alone are the Accepter of repentance, the Most Merciful. Our Lord, raise among them a messenger from their own people who will recite Your verses to them, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. You alone are the Almighty, the All-Wise.”

Thus Ishmael was a source of goodness and blessing for his father. His coming into the world, as the Qur’an explains, was itself the fulfillment of his father’s supplication:

> “My Lord, grant me a righteous child.” So We gave him the glad news of a forbearing son.

# 4. الأَبْوَابُ السِّتَّةُ لِلثُّلَاثِيِّ المُجَرَّدِ
## The Six Bābs of the Triliteral Unaugmented Verb

> Only a verb that is truly **ثُلَاثِيٌّ مُجَرَّدٌ** belongs to one of these six bābs.  
> Derived verbs such as **أَيَّدَ، وَافَقَ، سَجَّلَ، شَارَكَ، تَقَبَّلَ** do not belong to the six bābs in the forms used in this lesson.

| Traditional bāb name | Pattern | Standard example | Recognition rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| بَابُ نَصَرَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ | نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ | past middle vowel fatḥah; present middle vowel ḍammah |
| بَابُ ضَرَبَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ | past middle vowel fatḥah; present middle vowel kasrah |
| بَابُ فَتَحَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ | past middle vowel fatḥah; present middle vowel fatḥah |
| بَابُ سَمِعَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعَلُ | سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ | past middle vowel kasrah; present middle vowel fatḥah |
| بَابُ كَرُمَ | فَعُلَ يَفْعُلُ | كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ | past middle vowel ḍammah; present middle vowel ḍammah |
| بَابُ حَسِبَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعِلُ | حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ | past middle vowel kasrah; present middle vowel kasrah |

> The numerical order of the six bābs may differ between textbooks. Memorize the **name and vowel pattern**, not only a number.

### Important correction: **اِرْتَاحَ** versus **أَرَاحَ**

The worksheet’s page 5 gives:

- **اِرْتَاحَ – يَرْتَاحُ – اِرْتَحْ – اِرْتِيَاحٌ**
- Meaning: **to feel relieved or rested**
- Classification: **Form VIII — بَابُ الافْتِعَالِ**

However, the actual passage says:

| Past | Present | Command | Verbal noun | Meaning | Classification | Passage form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| أَرَاحَ | يُرِيحُ | أَرِحْ | إِرَاحَةٌ | to relieve or give rest to someone | **Form IV — بَابُ الإِفْعَالِ** | **وَأَرَاحَ قَلْبَ الأَبِ** = and He relieved the father’s heart |

Therefore:

- **اِرْتَاحَ الأَبُ** = the father felt relieved.
- **أَرَاحَ اللهُ قَلْبَ الأَبِ** = Allah relieved the father’s heart.

The second sentence is the construction actually used in the composition.

# 6. مُلَاحَظَاتٌ نَحْوِيَّةٌ وَصَرْفِيَّةٌ
## Important Grammar and Morphology Notes

### 1. **أَنْ يَذْبَحَهُ أَبُوهُ**

The verb **يَذْبَحَهُ** comes after **أَنْ**, so it is in the subjunctive:

- يَذْبَحُ = he slaughters
- أَنْ يَذْبَحَ = that he slaughter
- أَنْ يَذْبَحَهُ أَبُوهُ = that his father should slaughter him

The attached **ـهُ** means **him**, referring to Ishmael.

---

### 2. Active and passive forms of **ذَبَحَ**

- **يَذْبَحُ** = he slaughters
- **يُذْبَحُ** = he/it is slaughtered

The passage uses both:

- **أَنْ يَذْبَحَهُ أَبُوهُ** = that his father slaughter him
- **بِكَبْشٍ عَظِيمٍ يُذْبَحُ بَدَلَ الابْنِ** = with a great ram that would be slaughtered instead of the son

---

### 3. **يَدْعُوَانِ** is dual

- يَدْعُو = he calls/prays
- يَدْعُوَانِ = the two of them call/pray

In the passage, it refers to Abraham and Ishmael:

> **وَكَانَا يَدْعُوَانِ رَبَّهُمَا**

“And they both were praying to their Lord.”

---

### 4. Weak final verbs — الأَفْعَالُ النَّاقِصَةُ

The lesson contains several weak-final verbs:

| Past | Present | Command | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| جَزَى | يَجْزِي | اِجْزِ | نَاقِصٌ يَائِيٌّ |
| فَدَى | يَفْدِي | اِفْدِ | نَاقِصٌ يَائِيٌّ |
| دَعَا | يَدْعُو | اُدْعُ | نَاقِصٌ وَاوِيٌّ |
| أَرَى | يُرِي | أَرِ | irregular Form IV weak verb |

The final weak letter is removed or changed in the command.

---

### 5. **فَدَيْنَاهُ**

This word contains:

- **فَدَى** = he ransomed
- **نَا** = We
- **هُ** = him

Therefore:

> **فَدَيْنَاهُ** = We ransomed him.

---

### 6. **صَدَّقْتَ**

- **صَدَّقَ** = he confirmed
- **صَدَّقْتَ** = you, one male, confirmed

In the verse:

> **قَدْ صَدَّقْتَ الرُّؤْيَا**

“You have fulfilled or confirmed the vision.”

---

### 7. **أَرِنَا**

The base command is:

- **أَرِ** = show!

Adding **نَا** gives:

- **أَرِنَا** = show us!

> **وَأَرِنَا مَنَاسِكَنَا** = and show us our rites.

---

### 8. **مُسْلِمَيْنِ**

**مُسْلِمَيْنِ** is dual and refers to Abraham and Ishmael:

- مُسْلِمٌ = one submitting person
- مُسْلِمَانِ = two submitting people in the nominative
- مُسْلِمَيْنِ = two submitting people in the accusative or genitive

It is accusative after **اجْعَلْنَا**:

> **وَاجْعَلْنَا مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ**

“Make both of us submissive to You.”

# 7. خُطَّةُ الحِفْظِ وَالإِتْقَانِ فِي يَوْمٍ إِلَى ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ

Say each verb as a four-part chain:

- **دَعَا — يَدْعُو — اُدْعُ — دُعَاءٌ — بَابُ نَصَرَ**

- **ثُلَاثِيٌّ مُجَرَّدٌ**
- **مَزِيدٌ فِيهِ**

Without looking:

- Explain the difference between **اِرْتَاحَ** and **أَرَاحَ**.
