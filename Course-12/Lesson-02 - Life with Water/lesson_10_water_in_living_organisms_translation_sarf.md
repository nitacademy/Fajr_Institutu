# 2. التَّرْجَمَةُ الإِنْجِلِيزِيَّةُ الْكَامِلَةُ
## Complete Natural English Translation

Most of the bodies of living organisms consist of water. However, the amount of water in them varies according to the nature of their environment, their characteristics, and the different stages of their lives.

For example, water is present in small amounts in seeds, hooves, and horns. It is also relatively limited in some desert animals. In contrast, water accounts for more than ninety percent of the weight of certain fruits, such as tomatoes and cucumbers, as well as many marine organisms.

If we take the human being as an example, we find that approximately two-thirds of the human body is water. Water carries to every cell in the human body the substances necessary for its life, including oxygen, nourishment, hormones, immune substances, medicines, and vitamins. It also removes every harmful and toxic waste product from the cells.

All biological processes in the human body, without exception, take place only in the presence of water. Without water, respiration, nourishment, digestion, movement, excretion, and reproduction cannot occur.

Were it not for water, a human being would neither taste food nor smell fragrance. His tissues would dry out, his joints would stick together, and his body temperature would rise until he died.

# 4. الأَبْوَابُ السِّتَّةُ لِلثُّلَاثِيِّ الْمُجَرَّدِ
## The Six Bābs of the Triliteral Unaugmented Verb

> A verb belongs to one of these six bābs only when the form being studied is genuinely **ثُلَاثِيًّا مُجَرَّدًا**.  
> Derived verbs such as **تَفَاوَتَ، اتَّخَذَ، تَذَوَّقَ، تَيَبَّسَ، تَلَاصَقَ،** and **ارْتَفَعَ** do not belong to the six bābs in the forms used in this lesson.

| Traditional bāb | Pattern | Standard example | Recognition |
|---|---|---|---|
| بَابُ نَصَرَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ | نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ | fatḥah in the past middle radical; ḍammah in the present |
| بَابُ ضَرَبَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ | fatḥah in the past; kasrah in the present |
| بَابُ فَتَحَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ | fatḥah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ سَمِعَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعَلُ | سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ | kasrah in the past; fatḥah in the present |
| بَابُ كَرُمَ | فَعُلَ يَفْعُلُ | كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ | ḍammah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ حَسِبَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعِلُ | حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ | kasrah in both the past and present |

> Some ṣarf books number the six bābs differently. Memorize the **traditional name and vowel pattern**, not merely the bāb number.

# 6. مُلَاحَظَاتٌ نَحْوِيَّةٌ وَصَرْفِيَّةٌ
## Important Grammar and Morphology Notes

## 1. Non-human plural agreement

The passage says:

> **الْكَائِنَاتُ الْحَيَّةُ ... وَلَكِنَّهَا تَتَفَاوَتُ**

Although **الْكَائِنَاتُ** is plural, it refers to non-human beings collectively. Non-human plurals are commonly treated as feminine singular in Arabic agreement:

- **الْكَائِنَاتُ الْحَيَّةُ**
- **هِيَ**
- **تَتَفَاوَتُ**

This is why the passage does not say **يَتَفَاوَتُونَ**.

---

## 2. **بِحَسَبِ**

**بِحَسَبِ** means:

> according to; depending upon; in proportion to.

The passage gives three causes of variation:

1. **طَبِيعَةِ بِيئَتِهَا** — the nature of their environment  
2. **خَصَائِصِهَا** — their characteristics  
3. **أَطْوَارِ حَيَاتِهَا** — their stages of life  

---

## 3. **يَزِيدُ عَلَى التِّسْعِينَ فِي الْمِئَةِ**

This construction means:

> it exceeds ninety percent.

- **زَادَ عَلَى** = to exceed or be more than
- **التِّسْعِينَ** = ninety
- **فِي الْمِئَةِ** = percent

---

## 4. Conditional construction: **لَوِ اتَّخَذْنَا ... لَوَجَدْنَا**

This is a hypothetical conditional sentence:

- **لَوِ اتَّخَذْنَا** = if we took
- **لَوَجَدْنَا** = we would find

The **لَـ** in **لَوَجَدْنَا** introduces the answer to **لَوْ**.

---

## 5. **ثُلُثَيْ جِسْمِهِ**

**ثُلُثَيْ** means:

> two-thirds.

It is the dual form of **ثُلُثٌ** in the accusative/genitive form:

- ثُلُثٌ = one-third
- ثُلُثَانِ = two-thirds, nominative
- ثُلُثَيْنِ = two-thirds, accusative or genitive

When followed by **جِسْمِهِ**, the final nūn is removed because it is an iḍāfah:

> **ثُلُثَيْ جِسْمِهِ** = two-thirds of his body.

---

## 6. The pronoun in **يُخَلِّصُهَا**

The attached pronoun **هَا** refers most directly to **كُلِّ خَلِيَّةٍ**:

> **الْمَاءُ ... يُخَلِّصُهَا مِنْ كُلِّ نُفَايَةٍ**

“Water rids each cell of every waste product.”

---

## 7. **لَا تَجْرِي إِلَّا**

This is a restriction construction:

- **لَا** = not
- **إِلَّا** = except/only

> **لَا تَجْرِي إِلَّا فِي وُجُودِ الْمَاءِ**

“They take place only in the presence of water.”

---

## 8. **لَوْلَاهُ**

**لَوْلَا** means:

> if it were not for; were it not for.

The attached **ـهُ** refers to water:

> **وَلَوْلَاهُ** = and were it not for water.

The following verbs describe the unreal result:

- **مَا تَذَوَّقَ**
- **مَا شَمَّ**
- **لَتَيَبَّسَتْ**
- **تَلَاصَقَتْ**
- **ارْتَفَعَتْ**

---

## 9. The emphatic **لَـ** in **لَتَيَبَّسَتْ**

The **لَـ** introduces the result of **لَوْلَا** and emphasizes the consequence:

> **وَلَتَيَبَّسَتْ أَنْسِجَتُهُ**

“His tissues would certainly dry out.”

---

## 10. Feminine verb forms

The verbs are feminine singular because their subjects are grammatically feminine:

| Verb | Subject | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| تَيَبَّسَتْ | أَنْسِجَتُهُ | his tissues dried out |
| تَلَاصَقَتْ | مَفَاصِلُهُ | his joints stuck together |
| ارْتَفَعَتْ | دَرَجَةُ حَرَارَةِ جِسْمِهِ | his body temperature rose |

**أَنْسِجَةٌ** and **مَفَاصِلُ** are non-human plurals and therefore take feminine singular verbs. **دَرَجَةٌ** is itself feminine singular.

---

## 11. **حَتَّى يَمُوتَ**

After **حَتَّى** expressing an intended or resulting endpoint, the present verb is subjunctive:

- يَمُوتُ = he dies
- حَتَّى يَمُوتَ = until he dies

The final fatḥah shows that **يَمُوتَ** is منصوب.

---

## 12. The doubled verb **شَمَّ**

The root is:

> ش م م

Because the second and third root letters are identical, they merge:

- Original pattern: شَمَمَ
- Actual form: شَمَّ
- Present: يَشُمُّ
- Command: شُمَّ

It is therefore called:

> **فِعْلٌ مُضَاعَفٌ** — a doubled verb.
