# 2. التَّرْجَمَةُ الإِنْجِلِيزِيَّةُ الْكَامِلَةُ
## Complete Natural English Translation

The relationship between water and the human being is a long story. It begins while the human being is a drop of reproductive fluid floating in water and then a fetus in the womb of his mother. All the necessities of life reach him from his mother, carried by water.

Then, as a child, he receives his first nourishment by suckling milk from his mother’s breast—pleasant milk whose principal component is water. Water remains with the human being even in his pain and sorrow, which he sheds in the form of tears.

It is therefore not surprising that a human being can endure hunger for many days, while he cannot endure thirst for more than one day or a few days, usually no more than four.

A human being obtains the water he needs from three principal sources. Approximately 47 percent is consumed directly as water or as liquids of different compositions. Another 39 percent is found in what we call solid foods. Meat, vegetables, fruits, and bread all contain varying proportions of water.

The remaining 14 percent is produced as a result of metabolic processes taking place within the body. Regarding water that leaves the body, approximately two-thirds exits with urine; normal urine is about 95 percent water. The remaining one-third leaves through sweat, exhaled air, and the material expelled by the intestines.

> **Percentage memory aid:** 47% from drinks + 39% from solid food + 14% produced through bodily processes = 100%.

# 4. الأَبْوَابُ السِّتَّةُ لِلثُّلَاثِيِّ الْمُجَرَّدِ
## The Six Bābs of the Triliteral Unaugmented Verb

> A verb belongs to one of the six bābs only when it is genuinely **ثُلَاثِيًّا مُجَرَّدًا**.  
> All four verbs in the lesson’s verb table are triliteral mujarrad verbs.

| Traditional bāb | Pattern | Standard example | Recognition |
|---|---|---|---|
| بَابُ نَصَرَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ | نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ | fatḥah in the past middle radical and ḍammah in the present |
| بَابُ ضَرَبَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ | fatḥah in the past and kasrah in the present |
| بَابُ فَتَحَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ | fatḥah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ سَمِعَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعَلُ | سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ | kasrah in the past and fatḥah in the present |
| بَابُ كَرُمَ | فَعُلَ يَفْعُلُ | كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ | ḍammah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ حَسِبَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعِلُ | حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ | kasrah in both the past and present |

> Some ṣarf books number the bābs differently. Memorize the **traditional name and vowel pattern**, rather than relying only on a number.

# 6. مُلَاحَظَاتٌ نَحْوِيَّةٌ وَصَرْفِيَّةٌ
## Important Grammar and Morphology Notes

## 1. **تَبْدَأُ مَعَهُ نُطْفَةً**

The word **نُطْفَةً** describes the state in which the human story begins:

> It begins with him **as a drop**.

It may be understood as a circumstantial or developmental description.

---

## 2. **ثُمَّ جَنِينًا ... ثُمَّ طِفْلًا**

The passage omits a repeated verb because its meaning is understood:

- ثم يكون جنينًا = then he becomes a fetus
- ثم يكون طفلًا = then he becomes a child

Arabic often omits repeated words when the intended meaning is clear.

---

## 3. **مَحْمُولَةً مَعَ الْمَاءِ**

**مَحْمُولَةً** is the passive participle from:

> **حَمَلَ — يَحْمِلُ** = to carry.

It describes the necessities of life:

> **ضَرُورَاتُ الْحَيَاةِ ... مَحْمُولَةً مَعَ الْمَاءِ**

“The necessities of life reach him, carried with/by water.”

---

## 4. **لَبَنًا سَائِغًا قِوَامُهُ الْمَاءُ**

- **لَبَنًا** = milk
- **سَائِغًا** = pleasant and easy to swallow
- **قِوَامُهُ الْمَاءُ** = its basis or principal component is water

The adjective **سَائِغًا** agrees with **لَبَنًا** in gender, number, definiteness, and case.

---

## 5. **يَذْرِفُهَا دُمُوعًا**

The attached pronoun **ـهَا** refers to **أَحْزَانِهِ**:

> He sheds his sorrows as tears.

**دُمُوعًا** explains the form in which those sorrows are shed.

---

## 6. **أَنْ يَسْتَطِيعَ**

After **أَنْ**, the present verb is subjunctive:

- يَسْتَطِيعُ = he is able
- أَنْ يَسْتَطِيعَ = that he is able/to be able

The final fatḥah marks the subjunctive mood.

---

## 7. **لَا يَتَحَمَّلُ الظَّمَأَ إِلَّا**

This is a restriction construction:

- **لَا** = not
- **إِلَّا** = except/only

The meaning is:

> He can endure thirst only for one day or a few days.

---

## 8. **أَيَّامًا قَلَائِلَ**

**قَلَائِلَ** is an adjective describing **أَيَّامًا**:

- أَيَّامًا = days
- قَلَائِلَ = few

Both are indefinite, plural, and accusative.

---

## 9. **لَا تَزِيدُ عَلَى الْأَرْبَعَةِ**

The verb **زَادَ عَلَى** means:

> to exceed or be more than.

Therefore:

> **لَا تَزِيدُ عَلَى الْأَرْبَعَةِ**  
> They do not exceed four.

The understood noun is **أَيَّامٍ**.

---

## 10. **مَصَادِرَ رَئِيسِيَّةٍ**

**مَصَادِرَ** is a diptote, **مَمْنُوعٌ مِنَ الصَّرْفِ**, because it follows a broken-plural pattern such as **مَفَاعِل**.

After **مِنْ**, it appears with fatḥah rather than kasrah:

> **مِنْ ثَلَاثَةِ مَصَادِرَ رَئِيسِيَّةٍ**

---

## 11. **مُخْتَلِفًا قِوَامُهَا**

The intended meaning is:

> liquids whose composition or consistency differs.

A clearer expanded form would be:

> **سَوَائِلَ مُخْتَلِفًا قِوَامُهَا**  
> liquids with differing compositions.

---

## 12. **فِيمَا نُسَمِّيهِ بِالْأَغْذِيَةِ الصُّلْبَةِ**

- **فِيمَا** = in what
- **نُسَمِّيهِ** = we call it
- **بِالْأَغْذِيَةِ الصُّلْبَةِ** = solid foods

The attached pronoun **ـهِ** refers to the category being named.

---

## 13. **مِنْ ثُلُثَيْهِ**

This means:

> of its two-thirds/about two-thirds of it.

The dual form is:

- ثُلُثَانِ = two-thirds, nominative
- ثُلُثَيْنِ = two-thirds, accusative/genitive
- ثُلُثَيْهِ = two-thirds of it

The final nūn is removed because the word is attached to the pronoun **ـهِ** in an iḍāfah-like construction.

---

## 14. **تَطْرُدُهُ الْأَمْعَاءُ**

Although **الْأَمْعَاءُ** is plural, it is a non-human plural. Non-human plurals often take feminine singular verb agreement:

> **تَطْرُدُهُ الْأَمْعَاءُ**

“The intestines expel it.”

The attached **ـهُ** refers to the waste or water being expelled.

# 7. خُطَّةُ الْحِفْظِ وَالإِتْقَانِ فِي يَوْمٍ إِلَى ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ
## A One-to-Three-Day Memorization and Mastery Plan

> Strong short-term mastery is possible within one to three days. For permanent retention, review the lesson again after seven days and after thirty days.

---

## One-Day Intensive Plan

### Session 1 — Understand the passage, 35 minutes

Divide the lesson into five meaning units:

1. Water accompanies the human being from the earliest stage of life.
2. The fetus receives life’s necessities through water.
3. A child’s first food is milk, whose basis is water.
4. Human beings obtain water from drinks, food, and bodily processes.
5. Water leaves the body through urine, sweat, exhalation, and the intestines.

Read each unit aloud twice and explain it in English without looking.

---

### Session 2 — Learn the percentages, 20 minutes

Memorize the source percentages as:

> **47 — 39 — 14**

- **47%** from water and other liquids
- **39%** from solid foods
- **14%** produced by bodily processes

Then memorize the water-loss division:

- about **two-thirds** through urine
- about **one-third** through sweat, exhaled air, and the intestines

# 9. الْخُلَاصَةُ
## Core Lessons to Remember

1. **Water accompanies the human being from the earliest stage of development.**
2. **The fetus receives life’s necessities through water carried from the mother.**
3. **A child’s first nourishment is milk, whose main component is water.**
4. **The lesson presents three sources of bodily water: drinks, solid foods, and internal bodily processes.**
5. **Water leaves the body mainly through urine and also through sweat, exhalation, and the intestines.**
6. **All four verbs in the lesson table are triliteral mujarrad verbs belonging to Bāb Naṣara, Bāb Ḍaraba, or Bāb Fataḥa.**
