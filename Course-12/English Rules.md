## 1. The "Nun" of Protection / Noon Al-Wiqayah (Grammar / Nahw)

This rule governs the extra letter $ن$ (Nun) that is placed between a word and the First-Person Singular Attached Pronoun (Yaa Al-Mutakallim / "me" or "my"). Its application varies between being mandatory, permissible, or forbidden[cite: 6].

### Explanation and Rule:
* **Definition:** *Noon Al-Wiqayah* is an extra "Nun" inserted immediately before the First-Person pronoun $ي$ (Yaa)[cite: 6].
* **Reason for Naming:** It is called the "Nun of Protection" because it protects the verb from receiving a *Kasrah* (the genitive vowel/under-mark), which is exclusive to nouns and never occurs on verbs[cite: 6].

### Classifications and Rulings:
1. **Verbs of All Tenses (Past, Present, and Imperative):** It is **mandatory** (*Wajib*) to insert the Nun (e.g., $خَلَقَنِي$ *khalaqa-ni* / He created me, $يَهْدِينِ$ *yahdee-ni* / He guides me, $فَاعْبُدْنِي$ *fa'bud-ni* / so worship me)[cite: 6].
2. **Prepositions ($مِنْ$ / "From" and $عَنْ$ / "About"):** It is **mandatory** (*Wajib*) to insert the Nun for these two specific prepositions (e.g., $مِنِّي$ *min-nee* / from me, $عَنِّي$ *'an-nee* / about me)[cite: 6].
3. **Inna and Its Sisters ($إِنَّ وَأَخَوَاتُهَا$):** It is **permissible** (*Jaa'iz*) to use or omit it (e.g., $إِنَّنِي$ / $إِنِّي$ *innanee* / *innee*). However, it is highly preferred with $لَيْتَ$ (e.g., $لَيْتَنِي$ *laytanee* / if only I) and rarely used with $لَعَلَّ$ (e.g., $لَعَلِّي$ *la'allee* / perhaps I)[cite: 6].

## 2. Infinitives of Triliteral Verbs / Masadir Al-Af'al Al-Thulathiyyah (Morphology / Sarf)

A *Masdar* (Infinitive/Verbal Noun) is a noun that denotes an action or event without being bound to a specific timeframe[cite: 6]. Triliteral (3-letter) verb infinitives are vast and primarily learned through listening/transmission (*Samaa'ee*), but there are predominant patterns based on the inherent meaning or connotation of the verb[cite: 6].

### Predominant Patterns Based on the Meaning of the Verb:

| Inherent Meaning of the Verb | Morphological Pattern (Weight) | Examples from Text |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Movement or Travel** | فَعِيلٌ (*Fa'eel*) | $رَحَلَ \leftarrow رَحِيلاً$ (To depart $\leftarrow$ departure), $دَبَّ \leftarrow دَبِيباً$ (To creep $\leftarrow$ creeping)[cite: 6] |
| **Sound / Voice** | فَعِيلٌ (*Fa'eel*) OR فُعالٌ (*Fu'aal*) | $نَعَقَ \leftarrow نَعِيقاً$ (To caw $\leftarrow$ cawing), $بَكَى \leftarrow بُكَاءً$ (To weep $\leftarrow$ weeping), $صَرَخَ \leftarrow صُرَاخاً$ (To shout $\leftarrow$ shouting)[cite: 6] |
| **Sickness / Disease** | فُعَالٌ (*Fu'aal*) | $سَعَلَ \leftarrow سُعَالاً$ (To cough $\leftarrow$ a cough), $زَكَمَ \leftarrow زُكَاماً$ (To catch a cold $\leftarrow$ a cold), $دَارَ \leftarrow دُوآراً$ (To feel dizzy $\leftarrow$ dizziness)[cite: 6] |
| **Refusal / Abstinence** | فِعَالٌ (*Fi'aal*) | $أَبَى \leftarrow إِبَاءً$ (To refuse $\leftarrow$ refusal), $نَفَرَ \leftarrow نِفَاراً$ (To bolt $\leftarrow$ aversion), $فَرَّ \leftarrow فِرَاراً$ (To flee $\leftarrow$ flight)[cite: 6] |
| **Profession / Craft** | فِعَالَةٌ (*Fi'aalah*) | $زَرَعَ \leftarrow زِرَاعَةً$ (To farm $\leftarrow$ agriculture), $تَجَرَ \leftarrow تِجَارَةً$ (To trade $\leftarrow$ commerce), $صَاغَ \leftarrow صِيَاغَةً$ (To smith/craft $\leftarrow$ smithing)[cite: 6] |
| **Color** | فُعْلَةٌ (*Fu'lah*) | $حَمُرَ \leftarrow حُمْرَةً$ (To turn red $\leftarrow$ redness), $صَفُرَ \leftarrow صُفْرَةً$ (To turn yellow $\leftarrow$ yellowness), $زَرُقَ \leftarrow زُرْقَةً$ (To turn blue $\leftarrow$ blueness)[cite: 6] |
| **Agitation / Commotion** | فَعَلانٌ (*Fa'alaan*) | $غَلَى \leftarrow غَلَيَاناً$ (To boil $\leftarrow$ boiling), $هَاجَ \leftarrow هَيَجَاناً$ (To surge $\leftarrow$ upheaval), $فَاضَ \leftarrow فَيْضَانا$ (To overflow $\leftarrow$ flooding)[cite: 6] |

### Predominant Patterns for General Verbs (No Specific Connotation):
* **فَعُلَ (*Fa'ula*) - Intransitive:** Its *Masdar* usually falls on **فُعُولَةٌ** (*Fu'oolah*) or **فَعَالَةٌ** (*Fa'aalah*) (e.g., $سَهُلَ \leftarrow سُهُولَةً$ / To be easy $\leftarrow$ easiness, $فَصُحَ \leftarrow فَصَاحَةً$ / To be eloquent $\leftarrow$ eloquence)[cite: 6].
* **فَعِلَ (*Fa'ila*) - Intransitive:** Its *Masdar* falls on **فَعَلٌ** (*Fa'al*) (e.g., $فَرِحَ \leftarrow فَرَحاً$ / To rejoice $\leftarrow$ joy, $عَطِشَ \leftarrow عَطَشاً$ / To be thirsty $\leftarrow$ thirst)[cite: 6].
* **فَعَلَ (*Fa'ala*) - Intransitive:** Its *Masdar* falls on **فُعُولٌ** (*Fu'ool*) (e.g., $جَلَسَ \leftarrow جُلُوساً$ / To sit $\leftarrow$ sitting, $قَعَدَ \leftarrow قُعُوداً$ / To sit/reside $\leftarrow$ seating)[cite: 6].
* **فَعِلَ (*Fa'ila*) & فَعَلَ (*Fa'ala*) - Transitive:** Their *Masdar* falls on **فَعْلاً** (*Fa'lan*) (e.g., $نَصَرَ \leftarrow نَصْراً$ / To aid $\leftarrow$ aid/victory, $فَهِمَ \leftarrow فَهْماً$ / To understand $\leftarrow$ understanding)[cite: 6].

## 3. Infinitives of Quadriliteral Verbs / Masadir Al-Af'al Al-Ruba'iyyah (Morphology / Sarf)

All infinitives of 4-letter verbs are **rule-based/analogical** (*Qiyaasee*). Their standard patterns depend directly on the form of the past tense verb and whether the core letters are sound or weak[cite: 6].

### Breakdown of Quadriliteral Infinitive Patterns:

1. **Verbs on the pattern of (أَفْعَلَ - *Af'ala*):**
   * **Sound Middle Radical (Sound Eye):** The *Masdar* is on the pattern **إِفْعَال** (*If'aal*) (e.g., $أَكْرَمَ \leftarrow إِكْرَاماً$ / To honor $\leftarrow$ honoring, $أَقْدَمَ \leftarrow إِقْدَاماً$ / To advance $\leftarrow$ advancement)[cite: 6].
   * **Weak Middle Radical (Hollow Eye):** The middle weak letter is dropped, and a $ة$ (Taa Marbutah) is added at the end, resulting in the pattern **إِقَالَة / إِفَعْلَة** (*If'alah* / *Iqaalah*) (e.g., $أَقَامَ \leftarrow إِقَامَةً$ / To establish $\leftarrow$ establishment, $أَبَانَ \leftarrow إِبَانَةً$ / To clarify $\leftarrow$ clarification)[cite: 6].

2. **Verbs on the pattern of (فَعَّلَ - *Fa''ala*):**
   * **Sound End Radical:** The *Masdar* is on the pattern **تَفْعِيل** (*Taf'eel*) (e.g., $سَلَّمَ \leftarrow تَسْلِيماً$ / To greet $\leftarrow$ greeting, $كَلَّمَ \leftarrow تَكْلِيماً$ / To speak to $\leftarrow$ speaking)[cite: 6].
   * **Weak End Radical (Defective):** The $ي$ of the *Taf'eel* pattern is removed and replaced with a final $ة$ (Taa Marbutah), yielding the pattern **تَفْعِلَة** (*Taf'ilah*) (e.g., $وَصَّى \leftarrow تَوْصِيَةً$ / To recommend $\leftarrow$ recommendation, $زَكَّى \leftarrow تَزْكِيَةً$ / To purify $\leftarrow$ purification)[cite: 6].

3. **Verbs on the pattern of (فَاعَلَ - *Faa'ala*):**
   * The *Masdar* rule allows for two valid standard weights: **فِعَال** (*Fi'aal*) or **مُفَاعَلَة** (*Mufaa'alah*) (e.g., $قَاتَلَ \leftarrow قِتَالاً\ \text{أو}\ مُقَاتَلَةً$ / To fight $\leftarrow$ fighting/combat, $خَاصَمَ \leftarrow خِصَاماً\ \text{أو}\ مُخَاصَمَةً$ / To dispute $\leftarrow$ dispute)[cite: 6].

4. **Verbs on the pattern of (فَعْلَلَ - *Fa'lala*):**
   * The basic *Masdar* is on the pattern **فَعْلَلَة** (*Fa'lalah*) (e.g., $دَحْرَجَ \leftarrow دَحْرَجَةً$ / To roll $\leftarrow$ rolling)[cite: 6].
   * **Reduplicated Quadriliteral Verbs** (where the 1st & 3rd letters are identical, and 2nd & 4th letters are identical) can also optionally take the pattern **فِعْلَال** (*Fi'laal*) (e.g., $زَلْزَلَ \leftarrow زَلْزَلَةً\ \text{أو}\ زِلْزَالاً$ / To shake $\leftarrow$ earthquake/shaking)[cite: 6].

## 4. Infinitives of 5-Letter & 6-Letter Verbs / Masadir Al-Af'al Al-Khumasiyyah wal-Sudasiyyah (Morphology / Sarf)

The infinitives of 5-letter (Khumasi) and 6-letter (Sudasi) verbs are strictly **rule-based** (*Qiyaasee*). Their structure is governed systematically by the prefix or starting letter of the past-tense verb[cite: 6].

### Standard Structural Rules:

* **Verbs Starting with an Extra Taa ($ت$):** The *Masdar* is identical to the past tense form, with the simple adjustment of putting a **Dammah (u-vowel) on the second-to-last letter**[cite: 6].
  * *5-Letter Examples:* $تَعَلَّمَ \leftarrow تَعَلُّماً$ (To learn $\leftarrow$ learning), $تَقَدَّمَ \leftarrow تَقَدُّماً$ (To progress $\leftarrow$ progress), $تَنَافَسَ \leftarrow تَنَافُساً$ (To compete $\leftarrow$ competition)[cite: 6].

* **Verbs Starting with a Connecting Hamzah (Hamzat Wasl - $ا$):** The *Masdar* is formed by **breaking (adding a Kasrah to) the 3rd letter and adding an Alif ($ا$) before the final letter**[cite: 6].
  * *5 & 6-Letter Examples:* $اشْتَدَّ \leftarrow اشْتِدَاداً$ (To intensify $\leftarrow$ intensification), $اصْفَرَّ \leftarrow اصْفِرَاراً$ (To turn yellow $\leftarrow$ yellowness), $اسْتَكْبَرَ \leftarrow اسْتِكْبَاراً$ (To be arrogant $\leftarrow$ arrogance), $اطْمَأَنَّ \leftarrow اطْمِئْناناً$ (To be reassured $\leftarrow$ tranquility)[cite: 6].

* **Exception for Hollow Verbs on the pattern (اسْتَفْعَلَ - *Istaf'ala*):** If the middle radical of a 6-letter verb is weak (an Alif), the Alif of the *Istif'aal* pattern is omitted, and a final $ة$ (Taa Marbutah) is added in compensation, resulting in the pattern **اسْتِفَالَة** (*Istifaalah*)[cite: 6].
  * *Examples:* $اسْتَقَامَ \leftarrow اسْتِقَامَةً$ (To be upright $\leftarrow$ uprightness), $اسْتَعَانَ \leftarrow اسْتِعَانَةً$ (To seek help $\leftarrow$ assistance), $اسْتَفَادَ \leftarrow اسْتِفَادَةً$ (To benefit $\leftarrow$ utilization)[cite: 6].
