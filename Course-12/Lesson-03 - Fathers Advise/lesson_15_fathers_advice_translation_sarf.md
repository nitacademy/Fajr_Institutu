# 3. التَّرْجَمَةُ الإِنْجِلِيزِيَّةُ الْكَامِلَةُ
## Complete Natural English Translation

A father advised his daughter on the night of her wedding and said:

“My daughter, marriage is not merely an outing or a source of entertainment. Rather, it is a great responsibility: the responsibility of caring for the affairs of an entire family. It begins with attending to the affairs of one’s partner in the journey of life. It then soon extends to include sons and daughters, and later grandchildren.

Marriage includes the responsibility of raising the sons and daughters of the Muslim community. Home education and upbringing play a major role in giving the community its identity and preserving its existence, structure, and integrity.”

Some girls marry because they want to escape the restrictions imposed by their fathers. They imagine that marriage is a life completely free from restrictions. This is a very mistaken assumption. Parents generally do not impose restrictions that are opposed to the interests and happiness of their daughters.

This is true of the overwhelming majority of people, and an exceptional case is not treated as the general rule. A life entirely free of restrictions cannot exist. Absolute freedom results in harm and destruction.

Rather, a young woman should understand that she generally has greater freedom while living in her father’s home than she may have after moving into her husband’s home.

# 5. الأَبْوَابُ السِّتَّةُ لِلثُّلَاثِيِّ الْمُجَرَّدِ
## The Six Bābs of the Triliteral Unaugmented Verb

> A verb belongs to one of these six bābs only when the form being studied is genuinely **ثُلَاثِيًّا مُجَرَّدًا**.  
> The verb **وَصَّى** is a derived Form II verb, so it does not belong to one of the six mujarrad bābs in the form used in this lesson.

| Traditional bāb | Pattern | Standard example | Recognition |
|---|---|---|---|
| بَابُ نَصَرَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ | نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ | fatḥah in the past middle radical and ḍammah in the present |
| بَابُ ضَرَبَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ | fatḥah in the past and kasrah in the present |
| بَابُ فَتَحَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ | fatḥah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ سَمِعَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعَلُ | سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ | kasrah in the past and fatḥah in the present |
| بَابُ كَرُمَ | فَعُلَ يَفْعُلُ | كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ | ḍammah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ حَسِبَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعِلُ | حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ | kasrah in both the past and present |

> Some ṣarf books number these bābs differently. Memorize the **traditional name and vowel pattern**, not only the number.

# 7. مُلَاحَظَاتٌ نَحْوِيَّةٌ وَصَرْفِيَّةٌ
## Important Grammar and Morphology Notes

## 1. **وَصَّى أَبٌ ابْنَتَهُ**

The verb is Form II:

- **وَصَّى** = he advised
- **أَبٌ** = a father, the subject
- **ابْنَتَهُ** = his daughter, the direct object

The suffixes in **ابْنَتَهُ** are:

- **ابْنَةَ** = daughter
- **ـهُ** = his

---

## 2. **لَيْلَةَ الزَّوَاجِ**

This is an expression of time:

> on the night of the marriage/wedding.

**لَيْلَةَ** is accusative because it functions as **ظَرْفُ زَمَانٍ**, an adverb of time.

---

## 3. **لَيْسَ نُزْهَةً**

After **لَيْسَ**:

- its noun is normally nominative,
- its predicate is accusative.

Here:

- **الزَّوَاجَ** is the noun of **إِنَّ** before **لَيْسَ**
- the hidden pronoun in **لَيْسَ** refers to marriage
- **نُزْهَةً** is the accusative predicate of **لَيْسَ**

Meaning:

> Marriage is not an outing.

---

## 4. **وَإِنَّمَا هُوَ مَسْؤُولِيَّةٌ**

**إِنَّمَا** creates restriction and emphasis:

> Marriage is not merely entertainment; rather, it is a responsibility.

A natural translation is:

> It is nothing but a great responsibility.

---

## 5. **لَا تَلْبَثُ أَنْ تَشْمَلَ**

The expression:

> **لَا يَلْبَثُ أَنْ يَفْعَلَ**

means:

> it soon does something; it is not long before it does something.

Therefore:

> **لَا تَلْبَثُ أَنْ تَشْمَلَ الْأَبْنَاءَ وَالْبَنَاتِ**

“It soon extends to include sons and daughters.”

---

## 6. **إِنَّهَا مَسْؤُولِيَّةُ تَرْبِيَةِ...**

The pronoun **هَا** refers to marriage and its responsibilities.

The construction is an iḍāfah:

- **مَسْؤُولِيَّةُ تَرْبِيَةِ**
- the responsibility of raising/educating

---

## 7. **لِلتَّرْبِيَةِ الْمَنْزِلِيَّةِ دَوْرًا كَبِيرًا**

The prepositional phrase comes first:

- **لِلتَّرْبِيَةِ الْمَنْزِلِيَّةِ** = home education has/for home education
- **دَوْرًا كَبِيرًا** = a major role

Because the sentence follows **إِنَّ**, **دَوْرًا** is accusative.

---

## 8. **بَعْضُ الْبَنَاتِ يَتَزَوَّجْنَ**

**يَتَزَوَّجْنَ** ends with **نُونُ النِّسْوَةِ**, the feminine plural ending:

- يَتَزَوَّجُ = he marries
- يَتَزَوَّجْنَ = they, females, marry

The subject is:

> **بَعْضُ الْبَنَاتِ** — some girls.

---

## 9. **تَخَلُّصًا مِنْ قُيُودِ آبَائِهِنَّ**

**تَخَلُّصًا** is a maṣdar used to express purpose:

> They marry **in order to escape** their fathers’ restrictions.

This is often called:

> **الْمَفْعُولُ لِأَجْلِهِ** — the object expressing reason or purpose.

---

## 10. **مُتَصَوِّرَاتٍ**

This is a feminine plural active participle from:

> **تَصَوَّرَ — يَتَصَوَّرُ**

It functions as **حَالٌ**, describing their state while marrying:

> while imagining or assuming.

It is accusative because the circumstantial expression **الحال** is accusative.

---

## 11. **حَيَاةٌ تَخْلُو مِنَ الْقُيُودِ**

The verb **خَلَا مِنْ** means:

> to be free of; to contain none of; to be devoid of.

Examples:

- **الْبَيْتُ يَخْلُو مِنَ النَّاسِ** = The house is empty of people.
- **حَيَاةٌ تَخْلُو مِنَ الْقُيُودِ** = A life free from restrictions.

---

## 12. **لَا قُيُودَ عِنْدَهُمْ**

This uses **لَا النَّافِيَةُ لِلْجِنْسِ**, which completely negates the existence of something:

> There are no restrictions with them that oppose their daughters’ interests.

**قُيُودَ** is accusative after this **لَا**.

---

## 13. **الشَّاذُّ لَا حُكْمَ لَهُ**

This means:

> The exceptional case is not treated as the general rule.

It teaches that unusual or rare examples should not be used to judge the overwhelming majority.

---

## 14. **أَنْ تُوجَدَ حَيَاةٌ خَالِيَةٌ**

The verb **تُوجَدَ** is passive and subjunctive after **أَنْ**:

- تُوجَدُ = exists/is found
- أَنْ تُوجَدَ = that there exist

**حَيَاةٌ** is the نائب الفاعل, the passive subject.

---

## 15. Comparative expression

The passage says:

> **أَنَّهَا أَكْثَرُ حُرِّيَّةً عِنْدَمَا تَكُونُ فِي بَيْتِ أَبِيهَا، مِنْهَا عِنْدَمَا تَنْتَقِلُ إِلَى بَيْتِ زَوْجِهَا**

The intended comparison is:

> She is more free in her father’s home than after moving to her husband’s home.

A more direct comparative construction could also be expressed as:

> **هِيَ أَكْثَرُ حُرِّيَّةً فِي بَيْتِ أَبِيهَا مِنْهَا فِي بَيْتِ زَوْجِهَا.**

---

## 16. **خَلَا — يَخْلُو**

This is a weak-final verb:

- Root: خ ل و
- Past: خَلَا
- Present: يَخْلُو
- Command: اُخْلُ
- Maṣdar: خُلُوٌّ

It is called:

> **نَاقِصٌ وَاوِيٌّ** — a defective verb whose final radical is wāw.

---

# 8. خُطَّةُ الْحِفْظِ وَالإِتْقَانِ فِي يَوْمٍ إِلَى ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ
## A One-to-Three-Day Memorization and Mastery Plan

> Strong short-term mastery is possible within one to three days. For lasting retention, review the lesson after seven days and again after thirty days.

---

## One-Day Intensive Plan

### Session 1 — Understand the passage, 35 minutes

Divide the lesson into five ideas:

1. Marriage is a responsibility, not merely entertainment.
2. A wife begins by caring for the affairs of her life partner.
3. Responsibility later includes children and grandchildren.
4. Home upbringing protects the identity of the community.
5. Absolute freedom does not exist and can lead to harm.

Read each unit twice in Arabic, then explain it in English without looking.

---

### Session 2 — Vocabulary, 25 minutes

Group the vocabulary:

#### Marriage and family
- نُزْهَةٌ
- هُوِيَّةٌ
- كِيَانٌ

#### Rules and assumptions
- قُيُودٌ
- مُتَصَوِّرَاتٌ

#### Results
- دَمَارٌ

For each word, recall:

1. its English meaning,
2. its plural or singular,
3. its Arabic explanation,
4. one sentence from the composition.

---

### Session 3 — Verbs and ṣarf, 25 minutes

Recite the three verb chains:

- **وَصَّى — يُوَصِّي — وَصِّ — تَوْصِيَةٌ — Form II**
- **بَدَأَ — يَبْدَأُ — اِبْدَأْ — بَدْءٌ / بِدَايَةٌ — بَابُ فَتَحَ**
- **خَلَا — يَخْلُو — اُخْلُ — خُلُوٌّ — بَابُ نَصَرَ**

Then recall their exact forms:

- وَصَّى
- تَبْدَأُ
- تَخْلُو

---

### Session 4 — Grammar review, 30 minutes

Practice these five structures:

1. **لَيْسَ نُزْهَةً**
2. **إِنَّمَا هُوَ مَسْؤُولِيَّةٌ**
3. **لَا تَلْبَثُ أَنْ تَشْمَلَ**
4. **تَخَلُّصًا مِنْ قُيُودِ**
5. **مُتَصَوِّرَاتٍ أَنَّ...**

Explain the grammatical purpose of each expression.

## Five-Minute Review Cycle

Repeat this cycle several times:

1. Read three lines aloud.
2. Close the notes.
3. Explain the lines in English.
4. Recall three vocabulary words.
5. Conjugate one verb.
6. Explain one grammar structure.
7. Check and correct mistakes immediately.

Spend most of your study time on the items you fail to recall.

## Core Lessons to Remember

1. **Marriage is a serious responsibility rather than merely entertainment or personal freedom.**

3. **Home upbringing plays a major role in preserving the identity and integrity of the community.**
4. **Marriage should not be viewed simply as an escape from parental restrictions.**
5. **No human life is entirely free from rules and responsibilities.**
