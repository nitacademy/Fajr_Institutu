# 3. التَّرْجَمَةُ الإِنْجِلِيزِيَّةُ الْكَامِلَةُ
## Complete Natural English Translation

“My daughter, marriage is not continuous rest and sleep. Rather, it requires work and planning.

A successful wife is one who works for many hours in her home. Within the ‘kingdom’ of the household, she fulfills several important roles. She acts as a finance minister by managing the household budget together with her husband. She acts as a minister of internal affairs by protecting the security and stability of the home. She also acts as a minister of education and upbringing by raising her children, guiding them, and instilling in them noble feelings such as love for others and cooperation with them.

She is also responsible for household provisions, arranging food and clothing. She cooperates with her husband in organizing all these affairs, and she should not neglect any one of them.

My daughter, take a useful lesson from what we see and hear. We hear of many cases of divorce involving young women. Each one married while assuming that marriage meant going to parks, visiting friends every day, strolling through the markets every night, having a luxurious dinner in a large hotel every week, and traveling to Europe, Asia, America, and other places every year.

They also imagined that married life consisted of watching television programs, listening to radio broadcasts, speaking to friends on the telephone, wearing the finest and newest clothes, wearing the best jewelry from time to time, staying up late at women’s clubs and family gatherings, riding in the most beautiful cars, employing housemaids and cooks, and living in the most beautiful homes.”

# 5. الأَبْوَابُ السِّتَّةُ لِلثُّلَاثِيِّ الْمُجَرَّدِ
## The Six Bābs of the Triliteral Unaugmented Verb

> A verb belongs to one of these six bābs only when the form being studied is genuinely **ثُلَاثِيًّا مُجَرَّدًا**.  
> In this lesson, **غَرَسَ** is mujarrad, while **تَوَلَّى، رَبَّى،** and **دَبَّرَ** are derived forms.

| Traditional bāb | Pattern | Standard example | Recognition |
|---|---|---|---|
| بَابُ نَصَرَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعُلُ | نَصَرَ يَنْصُرُ | fatḥah in the past middle radical and ḍammah in the present |
| بَابُ ضَرَبَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعِلُ | ضَرَبَ يَضْرِبُ | fatḥah in the past and kasrah in the present |
| بَابُ فَتَحَ | فَعَلَ يَفْعَلُ | فَتَحَ يَفْتَحُ | fatḥah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ سَمِعَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعَلُ | سَمِعَ يَسْمَعُ | kasrah in the past and fatḥah in the present |
| بَابُ كَرُمَ | فَعُلَ يَفْعُلُ | كَرُمَ يَكْرُمُ | ḍammah in both the past and present |
| بَابُ حَسِبَ | فَعِلَ يَفْعِلُ | حَسِبَ يَحْسِبُ | kasrah in both the past and present |

> Some ṣarf books number the six bābs differently. Memorize the **traditional name and vowel pattern**, not only the number.

# 7. مُلَاحَظَاتٌ نَحْوِيَّةٌ وَصَرْفِيَّةٌ
## Important Grammar and Morphology Notes

## 1. **إِنَّ الزَّوَاجَ ... لَيْسَ رَاحَةً**

The sentence begins with **إِنَّ**:

- **الزَّوَاجَ** is the noun of **إِنَّ** and is accusative.
- The hidden pronoun in **لَيْسَ** refers to marriage.
- **رَاحَةً** and **نَوْمًا** are predicates of **لَيْسَ** and are accusative.

**مُتَوَاصِلًا** describes **نَوْمًا**.

---

## 2. **وَإِنَّمَا هُوَ عَمَلٌ وَتَخْطِيطٌ**

**إِنَّمَا** expresses restriction and emphasis:

> Marriage is not rest; rather, it is work and planning.

---

## 3. **بِضْعَ عَشْرَةَ سَاعَةً**

The expression **بِضْعَ عَشْرَةَ** normally indicates a number from thirteen to nineteen when used with a feminine counted noun such as **سَاعَةٌ**.

- **بِضْعَ عَشْرَةَ** = thirteen to nineteen
- **سَاعَةً** = hours, the counted noun

In natural English, the meaning may be conveyed as:

> many hours or more than ten hours.

---

## 4. Omitted repeated words

After **وَزِيرَةُ مَالِيَّةٍ**, the passage continues:

- **وَوَزِيرَةُ دَاخِلِيَّةٍ**
- **وَوَزِيرَةُ تَرْبِيَةٍ وَتَعْلِيمٍ**
- **وَوَزِيرَةُ تَمْوِينٍ**

The repeated meaning “she is” is understood and does not need to be stated each time.

---

## 5. **تَتَوَلَّى مَعَ زَوْجِهَا مِيزَانِيَّةَ الْبَيْتِ**

- **تَتَوَلَّى** = she takes charge of
- **مِيزَانِيَّةَ** = the direct object
- **الْبَيْتِ** = household, in an iḍāfah

The subject is a hidden feminine pronoun referring to the wife.

---

## 6. **تَغْرِسُ فِي نُفُوسِهِمُ الْعَوَاطِفَ**

The verb **غَرَسَ** literally means to plant.

In this passage, it is used figuratively:

> to instill noble feelings in their hearts.

This is similar to English expressions such as “planting values” or “instilling principles.”

---

## 7. **لَا يَجُوزُ لَهَا أَنْ تَتْرُكَ**

After **أَنْ**, the present verb is subjunctive:

- تَتْرُكُ = she leaves
- أَنْ تَتْرُكَ = to leave

**وَاحِدَةً مِنْهَا** means:

> any one of these responsibilities.

---

## 8. Feminine command **خُذِي**

The verb is:

- أَخَذَ = he took
- يَأْخُذُ = he takes
- خُذْ = take! to one male
- خُذِي = take! to one female

The father is addressing his daughter, so the feminine singular command is used.

---

## 9. **مِمَّا نَرَى وَنَسْمَعُ**

**مِمَّا** is formed from:

> **مِنْ + مَا**

It means:

> from what; from that which.

---

## 10. **حَوَادِثَ طَلَاقٍ**

**حَوَادِثَ** is a broken plural on a diptote pattern and therefore may take fatḥah where a regular noun would take kasrah.

The iḍāfah means:

> incidents or cases of divorce.

---

## 11. **تَزَوَّجَتِ الْوَاحِدَةُ مِنْهُنَّ**

- **تَزَوَّجَتْ** = she married
- **الْوَاحِدَةُ** = each individual woman
- **مِنْهُنَّ** = among them

The kasrah heard in **تَزَوَّجَتِ الْوَاحِدَةُ** helps avoid two consecutive silent letters when the words are joined.

---

## 12. A chain of verbal nouns

After **أَنَّ الزَّوَاجَ هُوَ**, the passage lists many verbal nouns:

- الذَّهَابُ
- زِيَارَةُ
- التَّجَوُّلُ
- الْعَشَاءُ
- السَّفَرُ
- مُشَاهَدَةُ
- سَمَاعُ
- التَّحَدُّثُ
- لُبْسُ
- السَّهَرُ
- رُكُوبُ
- اسْتِخْدَامُ
- السَّكَنُ

They describe what these young women mistakenly imagined married life to consist of.

---

## 13. Time expressions

The following are adverbs of time:

- **كُلَّ يَوْمٍ** = every day
- **كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ** = every night
- **كُلَّ أُسْبُوعٍ** = every week
- **كُلَّ عَامٍ** = every year

**كُلَّ** is accusative because it functions adverbially.

---

## 14. **أَفْضَلِ الْمَلَابِسِ وَأَحْدَثِهَا**

- **أَفْضَلِ الْمَلَابِسِ** = the best clothes
- **أَحْدَثِهَا** = the newest of them

The suffix **ـهَا** refers to the clothes.

---

## 15. **بَيْنَ الْحِينِ وَالْحِينِ**

This is an idiomatic expression meaning:

> from time to time; occasionally.

---

## 16. Weak verbs in the table

### **تَوَلَّى — يَتَوَلَّى**

This is a weak-final derived verb. Its command is:

> **تَوَلَّ**

### **رَبَّى — يُرَبِّي**

This is also a weak-final derived verb. Its command is:

> **رَبِّ**

The final weak letter is removed in the command.

# 8. خُطَّةُ الْحِفْظِ وَالإِتْقَانِ فِي يَوْمٍ إِلَى ثَلَاثَةِ أَيَّامٍ
## A One-to-Three-Day Memorization and Mastery Plan

> Strong short-term mastery is possible in one to three days. For long-term retention, review the lesson after seven days and again after thirty days.

---

## One-Day Intensive Plan

### Session 1 — Understand the passage, 35 minutes

Divide the lesson into five ideas:

1. Marriage requires work and planning.
2. Household management includes financial and internal responsibilities.
3. Raising children includes guidance and instilling noble values.
4. Spouses should cooperate in organizing family affairs.
5. Marriage should not be imagined as a life of constant entertainment and luxury.

Read each idea twice in Arabic and explain it in English without looking.

---

### Session 2 — Vocabulary, 30 minutes

Study the words in three groups:

#### Household organization
- تَخْطِيطٌ
- مِيزَانِيَّةٌ
- تَمْوِينٌ
- مَصْلَحَةٌ

#### Character and education
- الْعَوَاطِفُ
- مُتَوَاصِلٌ

#### Leisure and luxury
- التَّجَوُّلُ
- الْفَخْمُ
- الْحُلِيُّ
- النَّوَادِي

For each word, recall its meaning, opposite or singular/plural, and one phrase from the composition.

---

### Session 3 — Verbs and ṣarf, 30 minutes

Recite the verb chains:

- **تَوَلَّى — يَتَوَلَّى — تَوَلَّ — تَوَلٍّ — Form V**
- **رَبَّى — يُرَبِّي — رَبِّ — تَرْبِيَةٌ — Form II**
- **غَرَسَ — يَغْرِسُ — اِغْرِسْ — غَرْسٌ — بَابُ ضَرَبَ**
- **دَبَّرَ — يُدَبِّرُ — دَبِّرْ — تَدْبِيرٌ — Form II**

Then recall the exact forms used:

- تَتَوَلَّى
- تُرَبِّي
- تَغْرِسُ
- تُدَبِّرُ

---

### Session 4 — Grammar and production, 30 minutes

Practice:

1. **إِنَّ الزَّوَاجَ لَيْسَ رَاحَةً**
2. **وَإِنَّمَا هُوَ عَمَلٌ وَتَخْطِيطٌ**
3. **لَا يَجُوزُ لَهَا أَنْ تَتْرُكَ**
4. **خُذِي يَا ابْنَتِي دَرْسًا مُفِيدًا**
5. The list of verbal nouns after **أَنَّ الزَّوَاجَ هُوَ**

Retell the passage in five English sentences.

---

## Recommended Three-Day Plan

## Day 1 — Paragraph Three

- Read paragraph 3 aloud three times.
- Study its inline translation.
- Memorize the household-role vocabulary.
- Learn the four verb chains.
- Explain the four “minister” roles described in the lesson.

### Day 1 target

You should be able to explain the responsibilities related to finance, home security, education, and household provisions.

---

## Day 2 — Paragraph Four

- Read paragraph 4 aloud three times.
- Memorize the leisure and luxury vocabulary.
- Divide the long list into:
  - daily activities
  - weekly activities
  - yearly travel
  - clothing and jewelry
  - social activities
  - servants, cars, and homes
- Translate the paragraph without looking.

### Day 2 target

You should be able to explain the unrealistic understanding of marriage criticized by the passage.

---

## Day 3 — Ṣarf and Production

- Identify **غَرَسَ يَغْرِسُ** as **بَابُ ضَرَبَ**.
- Identify the three derived verbs.
- Translate the complete lesson without looking.
- Write six simple Arabic summary sentences.
- Complete blank vocabulary and verb tables from memory.

### Suggested Arabic summary sentences

1. **الزَّوَاجُ عَمَلٌ وَتَخْطِيطٌ، وَلَيْسَ رَاحَةً دَائِمَةً.**  
   Marriage requires work and planning and is not permanent rest.

2. **تَتَعَاوَنُ الزَّوْجَةُ مَعَ زَوْجِهَا فِي مِيزَانِيَّةِ الْبَيْتِ.**  
   The wife cooperates with her husband in managing the household budget.

3. **تُرَبِّي الْأُمُّ أَوْلَادَهَا وَتُوَجِّهُهُمْ.**  
   A mother raises and guides her children.

4. **تَغْرِسُ فِي نُفُوسِهِمْ حُبَّ الْآخَرِينَ وَالتَّعَاوُنَ.**  
   She instills in them love for others and cooperation.

5. **لَا يَجُوزُ إِهْمَالُ شُؤُونِ الْأُسْرَةِ.**  
   The affairs of the family must not be neglected.

6. **الزَّوَاجُ لَيْسَ سَفَرًا وَتَسَوُّقًا وَحَيَاةً مِنَ التَّرَفِ فَقَطْ.**  
   Marriage is not merely travel, shopping, and a life of luxury.

## Best Memorization Method

Use **active recall**, not passive rereading.

### Verb flashcard example

**Front:**

> غَرَسَ

**Back:**

> يَغْرِسُ — اِغْرِسْ — غَرْسٌ  
> بَابُ ضَرَبَ  
> Passage: **تَغْرِسُ فِي نُفُوسِهِمُ الْعَوَاطِفَ السَّامِيَةَ**

### Vocabulary flashcard example

**Front:**

> مِيزَانِيَّةٌ

**Back:**

> budget  
> Arabic explanation: **تَنْظِيمُ الْإِنْفَاقِ**  
> Passage: **مِيزَانِيَّةَ الْبَيْتِ**

---

## Five-Minute Review Cycle

Repeat this cycle several times:

1. Read three lines aloud.
2. Close the notes.
3. Explain the lines in English.
4. Recall three vocabulary words.
5. Conjugate one verb.
6. Name one household responsibility.
7. Check and correct mistakes immediately.

Spend most of your review time on the items you fail to recall.

# 10. الْخُلَاصَةُ
## Core Lessons to Remember

1. **Marriage requires work, planning, cooperation, and responsibility.**
2. **Household responsibilities include finances, security, education, food, and clothing.**
3. **Parents should guide children and instill noble emotions and cooperative values in them.**
4. **Spouses should cooperate in organizing all family affairs.**
5. **Marriage should not be understood merely as entertainment, travel, luxury, and social activity.**
6. **The verb table contains one mujarrad verb and three derived verbs.**
